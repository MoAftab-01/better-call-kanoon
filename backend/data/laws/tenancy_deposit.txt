Indian Contract Act, 1872 — Section 73 and Related Provisions on Security Deposits (Tenancy Context)

A landlord's obligation to return a security deposit upon termination of a tenancy is governed by the principles of contract and equity embedded in Indian law. The deposit serves as collateral for potential damage or unpaid rent during the lease term. Once the lease ends and the tenant has fulfilled all obligations — including payment of rent, settlement of utility bills, and restoration of the premises in the agreed condition — the landlord is bound by equity and contractual good faith to return the deposit promptly.

Under Section 73 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, compensation is recoverable for breach of contract. If a landlord withholds a security deposit without a legitimate claim for damages or unpaid dues, this constitutes a breach of the tenancy agreement. The tenant may seek restitution of the deposit amount along with interest from the date of wrongful retention, calculated at a reasonable rate reflecting the opportunity cost of the withheld sum.

In practice, courts have held that the burden of demonstrating legitimate deductions lies with the landlord. A mere assertion of damage without photographic evidence, repair estimates, or an itemized deduction list is generally insufficient. The deposit must be returned in full, with any deductions strictly proportionate and documented. Where the tenancy agreement is silent on the timeline for return, courts have applied a standard of "reasonable time" — typically within 30 to 60 days of handover — guided by customary practice and fairness.

The tenant should document the condition of the property at both the time of entry and exit using dated photographs and a signed handover checklist. This documentation serves as primary evidence in any dispute over deductions. In states where rent control or tenancy protection statutes apply (such as the Rent Control Acts of Maharashtra or Delhi), additional safeguards may exist that restrict arbitrary retention of deposits by landlords.
